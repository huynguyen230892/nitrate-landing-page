The catalog

The rest are still in copyright but are distributed under a licence that permits free viewing — a Creative Commons licence, or the rights holder's own grant. Where a title carries a licence, that licence travels with the film and its terms apply to you as a viewer.

We claim no copyright in the films themselves, and we did not scan, restore, or transfer them. Nothing in the catalog is sold, rented, or locked behind a payment.

Availability & condition of prints

These are surviving prints of films up to a century old, digitised by archivists and volunteers rather than by a studio restoration department. Density shifts, splices, missing frames, soft focus, and variable audio are inherent to the source elements. They are properties of the film, not defects in the service, and we do not apply AI sharpening or denoising to hide them.

Streaming depends on network conditions and on services outside our control. We aim for continuous availability but cannot guarantee uninterrupted streaming, and a title may become temporarily or permanently unavailable without notice.

Rights & takedown

We include titles only where our research indicates the film is in the public domain or is freely licensed for viewing. Research can be wrong, and rights differ by territory.

If you hold rights in a film listed in Nitrate and believe it should not be there, write to support@nitrate.info with the title, the basis of your claim, and the territory concerned. We will review it promptly and take the title out of the catalog while we do.
